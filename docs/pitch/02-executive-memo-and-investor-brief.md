## 2. The Market Opportunity & Structural Industry Flaws

The religious and faith-based institutional market in North America represents one of the largest, most underserved vertical software segments in the global economy:
* **Over 380,000 local congregations in the United States alone.**
* **More than $140 Billion in annual charitable tithes and offerings.**
* **14,000+ Christian day-schools, classical academies, and Bible colleges.**

Despite this scale, the market is severely hindered by two dominant industry dynamics:

### Flaw A: Private Equity Roll-Up Bloat (Pushpay / CCB)
Large private equity firms have acquired legacy software companies (Pushpay, Church Community Builder, Ministry Centered Technologies). Instead of innovating, they have hiked subscription prices, underinvested in core engineering, and locked churches into rigid multi-year contracts while operating disparate, clunky codebases.

### Flaw B: The "A La Carte" Module Tax (Planning Center)
While popular with tech-savvy churches, Planning Center unbundles every feature into a separate product. Pastors are forced to subscribe to seven separate apps (People, Giving, Services, Check-Ins, Registrations, Groups, Publishing). For a 600-person church, the monthly SaaS bill often exceeds $650–$900/month, yet it still fails to provide an integrated double-entry accounting ledger, an LMS for discipleship, or child safeguarding auditing.
